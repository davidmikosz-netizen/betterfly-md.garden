🚀 Start Small: Your Easy Lawn Conversion Guide

You don't need to dig up your whole yard overnight! Converting a small 4x4 pocket garden or a strip along your driveway creates an immediate pollinator sanctuary with minimal effort. Late summer through early fall is the ideal time to sheet mulch so cardboard and mulch smother grass and build soil over winter, while fall and early spring offer the best conditions for planting native perennials in Maryland.

1

Mow & Cardboard Layer

Mow your selected turf grass as short as possible. Cover the grass completely with plain brown cardboard (remove tape and staples). Overlap edges by 6 inches so grass can't sneak through!

2

Mulch & Compost

Cover cardboard with 3–4 inches of hardwood mulch or leaf compost. Water thoroughly. The cardboard smothers weeds while breaking down into rich soil earthworms love.

3

Plant Native Starters

Cut small holes through the cardboard layer to plant your native perennials. Water weekly during their first summer until their deep roots establish!

🌊 Protecting the Chesapeake Bay Watershed

Maryland's native flora evolved over thousands of years alongside our local soils, intense humidity, and unique rainfall patterns. Switching from turf lawn to native beds directly protects the Chesapeake Bay.

🌱

Deep Root Absorption

Traditional turf grass roots are only 2–3 inches deep. Native plants like Joe Pye Weed and Little Bluestem push roots 5–15 feet down into heavy Maryland clay, filtering stormwater runoff and preventing soil erosion.

🚫

Zero Synthetic Fertilizers

Lawns require nitrogen and phosphorus fertilizers that wash straight into stormwater drains during heavy summer thunderstorms, creating algae blooms in the Bay. Native plants thrive naturally without synthetic chemical inputs!

🦋

Specialist Insect Hosts

90% of plant-eating insects are host specialists—they can only reproduce on the specific native plants they co-evolved with. For example, Monarchs *must* have Milkweed, and the Baltimore Checkerspot *requires* White Turtlehead.

🦌 Deer & Bunny Defense Guide

Understanding who is munching on your garden is key to keeping your native plants safe without using harmful chemicals.

Feature 🦌 White-Tailed Deer 🐇 Eastern Cottontail Rabbit
Damage Sign Ragged, torn leaves & shredded stems (deer lack upper incisors). Damage up to 6 feet high. Clean, sharp 45-degree angle cuts on tender stems under 2 feet tall. Chewed woody bark in winter.
Physical Barriers Requires 8-foot tall fencing or 3D double fencing to disrupt leap perception. Requires 1/2-inch hardware cloth mesh. Must be buried 2–3 inches deep so they cannot dig underneath!
Repellent Type Smell-based repellents (putrescent egg solids, garlic, blood meal). Rotate brands monthly. Taste-based repellents (capsaicin/hot pepper spray). Reapply after heavy rainstorms!
Aromatic Screen Plants Mountain Mints, Aromatic Aster, Wild Bergamot, Spicebush, Fragrant Sumac. Mountain Mints, Giant Hyssop, Wild Bergamot, Golden Ragwort, Blue Mistflower.

💧 Hydrating the Sanctuary: Insect & Bird Water Stations

Maryland summers get hot and humid! Providing clean, safe water is just as important as supplying nectar for pollinators.

🐝 The Bee & Butterfly Puddle Station

Insects cannot swim in deep bird baths and will drown easily. Fill a shallow terracotta saucers with river pebbles, stones, or glass marbles. Add water so the stones remain half-exposed, giving bees and butterflies a stable landing pad to sip safely.

☀️ Solar Moving Water & Mosquito Prevention

Standing water breeds mosquitoes within 48 hours! Place a low-cost solar bubbler or fountain in your water dish. The moving water ripples keep mosquitoes from laying eggs while producing splashing sounds that lead migrating songbirds directly to your garden.
